# The N-of-1 Playbook

A step-by-step guide for the weeks and months after an ultra-rare diagnosis.

---

## Phase 1 — Get the facts straight

**1. Get the exact variant, in writing.**
Ask your genetics team for: the **gene**, the **exact variant** (e.g. `c.1072-1G>A`, `p.Leu39del`), the **zygosity**, and the **classification** (pathogenic / likely pathogenic / variant of uncertain significance). This precise string is the key that opens every registry and matching service below.

**2. Ask what the variant *does*.**
"Is this a loss-of-function, gain-of-function, or splicing variant?" The answer determines whether therapies like antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) are even conceptually possible.

**3. Request a clinical genetics referral** if you don't have one. If you already have one, ask when you should come back — testing improves, and a re-analysis after 18–24 months is often worth it.

**4. Consider whether you qualify for an undiagnosed-disease program.**
If there is still no answer, the Undiagnosed Diseases Network (https://undiagnosed.hms.harvard.edu/) exists for exactly this.

---

## Phase 2 — Find the other people

**5. Put your variant into a matching registry.**
Start with **MyGene2** (https://www.mygene2.org/) and **GenomeConnect** (https://www.genomeconnect.org/). These feed into **Matchmaker Exchange**, which searches many registries at once. Researchers use **GeneMatcher** (https://genematcher.org/).

**6. Search the communities by *gene* and by *symptom*.**
Search r/rarediseases and Facebook for the gene name, the syndrome name if one exists, and each major symptom. Many N-of-1 families find each other this way.

**7. If nothing exists, start it.**
A single well-written post — your story, the exact variant, what you're hoping to find — can travel further than you expect. DNA Unicorn (https://dnaunicorn.org/) publishes how-to guides for exactly this.

**8. Join the adjacent communities.**
Even without a disease-specific group, communities around epilepsy, hypotonia, mitochondrial disease, or immune dysregulation carry enormous practical knowledge that transfers.

---

## Phase 3 — Explore treatment

**9. Determine if the condition is a candidate for personalized therapy.**
Ask your specialist whether an **ASO** approach is plausible. This is the most established N-of-1 modality today.

**10. Approach the N-of-1 organizations.**
- **n-Lorem** (https://www.nlorem.org/) — free-for-life bespoke ASO therapies; usually needs a physician referral.
- **N=1 Collaborative** (https://www.n1collaborative.org/) — the network connecting these efforts.
- **Every Cure** (https://everycure.org/) — drug repurposing.

**11. If an approved therapy exists elsewhere, chase access.**
Contact the manufacturer directly and ask about a **compassionate-use** or **named-patient program**. Ask your clinician to make the approach on your behalf — it carries more weight.

**12. Search the trial landscape.**
Use ClinicalTrials.gov (https://clinicaltrials.gov/) to find trials, their principal investigators, and enrolling sites.

---

## Phase 4 — Sustain it

**13. Keep a symptom and intervention log.**
For an N-of-1 condition there is no natural-history data — *yours* is the data. A dated log of symptoms, treatments, and responses is genuinely valuable to researchers and clinicians.

**14. Protect your own wellbeing.**
The diagnostic odyssey is exhausting and isolating. Peer communities help; so does professional mental-health support. Rare-disease organizations increasingly provide it.

**15. Beware of false certainty.**
Ultra-rare conditions attract people selling unproven treatments. Prefer interventions with published evidence, regulatory oversight, and transparent risk.

---

## Quick-reference checklist

- [ ] Exact variant in writing (gene, variant, classification)
- [ ] Clinical genetics referral in place
- [ ] Listed in MyGene2 / GenomeConnect / Matchmaker Exchange
- [ ] Searching communities by gene and by symptom
- [ ] Asked whether an ASO / personalized therapy is plausible
- [ ] Contacted n-Lorem / N=1 Collaborative if relevant
- [ ] Checked ClinicalTrials.gov
- [ ] Symptom + treatment log started
