# N-of-1 & Ultra-Rare Disease Resources

A practical, plain-language guide for people facing an ultra-rare or "N-of-1" genetic diagnosis — and for the families, carers, and clinicians trying to help them.

This is a curated map of **communities, registries, and treatment pathways**. It is a starting point, not medical advice.

---

## What is an "N-of-1" condition?

An **N-of-1** (or *nano-rare*, or *ultra-rare*) condition is a genetic disease that has not been described in the medical literature before, or that is caused by a variant so rare it may affect only one person — or a handful — worldwide. Often it is known only by the name of the gene involved.

The numbers explain why it feels so isolating:

- There are roughly **7,000+ known rare diseases**, and the list keeps growing as more genomes are sequenced.
- Around **72% of rare diseases are genetic**, and most first appear in childhood.
- **~85% of rare diseases have a prevalence below 1 in 1,000,000** — the definitional home of N-of-1 cases.
- More than **95% of rare diseases have no approved specific treatment**.

A diagnosis like this usually arrives after a long, exhausting search. This guide is about what to do next.

---

## Start here: if you just received a diagnosis

1. **Get the exact variant in writing.** Ask for the gene name, the specific variant (e.g. `c.1072-1G>A`), and the classification (pathogenic / likely pathogenic / VUS). Everything below depends on having this precise string.
2. **Ask for a clinical genetics referral** if you don't already have one. This is the single highest-leverage step.
3. **Get your variant into a matching registry** (see [Registries & matching](#registries--matching)). Two families with the same variant can teach each other more than any textbook.
4. **Find your people** — even if there is no disease-specific group yet (see [Communities](#communities)).
5. **Check whether the condition is a candidate for a personalized therapy** (see [Treatment pathways](#treatment--therapy-pathways)).

A longer, step-by-step version lives in [`docs/playbook.md`](docs/playbook.md).

## Communities

When no one else has your condition, you build the community. These are the places people actually gather.

### General rare-disease forums

- **r/rarediseases** — https://www.reddit.com/r/rarediseases/ — the main hub. Runs recurring *Diagnostic Odyssey* megathreads for people still seeking a diagnosis.
- **r/biotech** — https://www.reddit.com/r/biotech/ — where families often ask about nano-rare treatment options and get pointed to the right organizations.
- **NORD** (National Organization for Rare Disorders) — https://rarediseases.org/
- **EURORDIS** (Europe) — https://www.eurordis.org/
- **Rare Portal** (Australia) — https://www.rareportal.org.au/

### When there is no disease-specific group

Don't wait for one to appear. Join communities built around **symptoms or systems** rather than the disease name — epilepsy, hypotonia, developmental delay, mitochondrial disease, immune dysregulation. Genetic conditions overlap heavily, and the practical advice (therapy, equipment, navigating school systems, advocacy) transfers.

### Building a group yourself

- **DNA Unicorn** — https://dnaunicorn.org/ — a resource built specifically for the N-of-1 community, including how-to guides for running your own search campaign across Reddit, Facebook, and elsewhere.
- Many N-of-1 communities start as a single post that goes viral. Bertrand Might's story is the canonical example: a father's essay spread through Reddit and connected families worldwide.

## Registries & matching

These services match patients by gene, variant, or symptoms. For an N-of-1 condition, this is often how you find the only other people on earth with your variant.

| Resource | What it does | Link |
|---|---|---|
| **MyGene2** | Patient portal; find others by gene or phenotype; feeds into Matchmaker Exchange | https://www.mygene2.org/ |
| **Matchmaker Exchange** | Federated network — one query searches many registries at once | https://www.matchmakerexchange.org/ |
| **GeneMatcher** | Researcher-to-researcher gene matching | https://genematcher.org/ |
| **GenomeConnect** | Share genetic results securely; get matched with others | https://www.genomeconnect.org/ |
| **Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN)** | NIH-funded network for people without a diagnosis | https://undiagnosed.hms.harvard.edu/ |
| **RARE-X** (Global Genes) | Structured patient-led data collection to accelerate research | https://rare-x.org/ |

## Treatment & therapy pathways

Most ultra-rare conditions have no approved treatment — but a small and growing number of organizations exist specifically to make **personalized, single-patient therapies** possible.

- **n-Lorem Foundation** — https://www.nlorem.org/ — develops bespoke antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) therapies for nano-rare patients and provides them **free for life**. Patients are typically referred by a physician at a tertiary-care center.
- **N=1 Collaborative** — https://www.n1collaborative.org/ — the international hub for individualized medicines; a good place to start when exploring the N-of-1 route.
- **1 Mutation 1 Medicine (1M1M)** — https://www.1m1m.org/ — European consortium building a best-practice framework for evaluating patients for tailored therapies.
- **Every Cure** — https://everycure.org/ — works on repurposing already-approved drugs for new diseases.
- **Mila's Miracle Foundation** — https://milasmiraclefoundation.org/ — the organization behind *milasen*, the first drug custom-made for a single person.
- **Lydian Accelerator** — https://www.lydianaccelerator.org/ — nonprofit building an open N-of-1 therapy platform.

### Practical routes when a therapy exists but isn't available to you

- **Contact the manufacturer directly.** Ask specifically about a **compassionate-use** or **named-patient program**.
- **Search ClinicalTrials.gov** — https://clinicaltrials.gov/ — to find the trial, its principal investigator, and the sites enrolling.
- **Ask your specialist to make the approach.** Clinician-to-company contact opens doors that patient emails often don't.

## Important cautions

- **This is not medical advice.** It is a directory. Every decision should go through a qualified clinician.
- **Peer communities are for support and lived experience, not diagnosis.** They are enormously valuable for the practical and emotional side — and unreliable as a source of clinical fact.
- **Beware of unproven treatments.** Rare-disease desperation attracts people selling certainty. Prefer interventions with published evidence and regulatory oversight.
- **For cookie/session-based platforms, use a secondary account** — not relevant here, but worth stating for any tooling you connect.
